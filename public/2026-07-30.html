Op-Eds & Think Tank Commentary
National Bureau of Asian Research (NBR)
Saori N. Katada and Kei Koga's Japan's Grand Strategy: Liminal Power in an Uncertain World
Japan occupies a 'liminal power' position — neither a great power nor a secondary state — that shapes its grand strategy in an era of US-China competition and global uncertainty.
Katada and Koga's NBR piece frames Japan's strategic posture as that of a 'liminal power,' caught between structural dependence on the US alliance and growing autonomy pressures driven by China's rise and Trump-era alliance uncertainty. The authors examine how Japan navigates this in-between status across security, economic, and diplomatic domains. The framework provides analytical vocabulary for understanding apparent inconsistencies in Tokyo's simultaneous defense buildup, fiscal stimulus, and diplomatic hedging.
The 'liminal power' framework helps US policymakers understand why Japan resists both full bandwagoning with US positions and independent balancing — a distinction relevant to current tariff, defense-burden, and China-policy negotiations.
IISS
Burden sharing beyond budgets: Japan's Official Security Assistance
Japan's Official Security Assistance (OSA) program represents a form of alliance burden-sharing that goes beyond raw defense budget numbers, extending Tokyo's security role to capacity-building in partner nations.
The IISS piece examines Japan's OSA program — Tokyo's grant-based security assistance to like-minded countries — as a distinct dimension of burden-sharing that is not captured in the 2%-of-GDP metric. The analysis charts how OSA allows Japan to project security influence across Southeast Asia and the Pacific without crossing constitutional redlines on arms transfers. The piece connects OSA to Japan's broader effort to demonstrate alliance value to Washington beyond host-nation support payments.
As US-Japan host-nation support negotiations approach the March 2027 SMA deadline, OSA's contribution to regional capacity-building provides Tokyo with additional leverage in framing its overall security contribution to the alliance.
