China-Japan

China imposes provisional anti-dumping measures on Japanese dichlorosilane; Japan protests

China's Commerce Ministry imposed provisional anti-dumping measures on Japanese dichlorosilane imports — a key chipmaking precursor chemical — effective Tuesday, requiring cash deposits of up to 99.2% from importers; Japan's government urged withdrawal and announced an assessment of semiconductor supply-chain impact. Dichlorosilane is used in the production of advanced semiconductors, making the measure a direct pressure point on Japan's chip industry and its alignment with US export controls on China.

So what: The measure targets a material central to Rapidus and broader Japanese semiconductor production, intersecting with Japan's economic-security cooperation with the US and ongoing US export-control alignment.

Pattern: China's use of anti-dumping measures against Japanese industrial materials follows a pattern of economic-coercion tools applied alongside diplomatic pressure, consistent with the reference data on China's sustained economic leverage campaign toward Japan.

BOJ set to hike to 1.25% next week as July wages post biggest gains in decades

Multiple reports project the Bank of Japan will raise its key interest rate by 25 basis points to 1.25% at next week's monetary policy meeting — returning to the highest level in 31 years — after July nominal wages surged 4.7% (the fastest pace in nearly 30 years) and real wages rose 2.4% (the biggest gain since 2021). US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent separately told BOJ Governor Kazuo Ueda that Washington supports efforts to strengthen the yen, per Asahi, aligning US policy signals with Japanese monetary tightening.

So what: A 1.25% BOJ rate and a yen near seven-month highs directly affect Japanese automakers' earnings guidance, carry-trade positions, and the timing of Japanese capital repatriation from US Treasuries.
