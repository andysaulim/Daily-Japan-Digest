Yen dashes across ¥153, strongest since February, as BOJ hike bets build

The yen strengthened from ¥160 to below ¥153 against the dollar in less than a week, its strongest level since February, on bets for faster BOJ rate hikes. July nominal wages surged 4.7% — the fastest in nearly 30 years — and Q2 GDP was revised up to 1.4% annualized, fueling September hike expectations.

So what: A September BOJ hike would compress the carry trade and hit exporter earnings, with Japanese carmakers already bracing for a profit hit from the stronger currency.

China imposes provisional anti-dumping duties on Japanese chip chemical

Beijing's Commerce Ministry required companies importing Japanese dichlorosilane to post cash deposits of up to 99.2% from Tuesday under provisional anti-dumping measures. Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara urged China to withdraw the measures and said Tokyo would assess the impact.

So what: The duties add an economic-coercion vector to the China-Japan relationship as Takaichi seeks a late-September Trump meeting timed around a prospective US-China summit.

North Korea commissions second nuclear-capable destroyer as trilateral drills open

Kim Jong Un said North Korea's newly commissioned second Choe Hyon-class guided-missile destroyer — the vessel that capsized on its May 2025 launch — is part of Pyongyang's nuclear response system and pledged a nuclear-armed navy. So what: The commissioning and drills raise the stakis for trilateral coordination and Japan's Nansei-facing defenses ahead of any Takaichi-Trump agenda.
