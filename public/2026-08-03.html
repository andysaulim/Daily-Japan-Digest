Japan and US conduct first joint yen-buying intervention in 15 years; further action signaled

Japan and the United States confirmed joint yen-buying intervention — the first in 15 years — with Finance Minister Katayama and Treasury Secretary Bessent both publicly warning that further action remains an option if the yen slides again. Trump stated the intervention was conducted at Japan's request and described it as 'a signal of friendship,' while the US executed purchases using euros to avoid dollar weakening, per Bloomberg strategists.

So what: The joint intervention and explicit further-action warnings by both finance ministers sets a new coordinated FX posture that will shape BOJ policy deliberations at its next meeting and US-Japan trade/monetary diplomacy in the near term.

Pattern: Kyodo News identifies this as the first joint US-Japan forex intervention in 15 years.

PM Takaichi visits quake-hit Kumamoto, meets victims; auto plant halt prolonged

PM Takaichi traveled to earthquake-affected Kumamoto Prefecture on August 3, meeting victims and local leaders to assess the recovery situation. An auto plant halt in the region was prolonged, furusato nozei donations reached ¥730 million, and a separate investigation found two mall workers were killed after their employer ordered them back inside before a fatal explosion.

So what: The PM's direct engagement with the Kumamoto disaster zone and the emerging employer-liability questions in the mall blast may shape emergency-response legislation and the political timeline for the quake-relief supplementary budget.

LDP virtually approves food consumption tax cut; opposition coalition remains fragmented

The LDP reached near-formal approval of its food consumption tax cut plan, advancing PM Takaichi's signature fiscal policy pledge toward Diet legislation. Simultaneously, local CDP members opposed a three-way merger with the CRA and Komeito, and the DPFP announced it will not join the ruling bloc, leaving the opposition fragmented.

So what: LDP's internal approval moves the food tax cut bill closer to a Diet vote, but a fragmented opposition reduces the prospect of a unified counter-proposal and increases the bill's passage likelihood.
