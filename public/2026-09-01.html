Japan's 10-year JGB hits 3% for first time in 30 years as Bessent presses BOJ rate hike at G20

Japan's benchmark 10-year government bond yield breached 3% on September 1 — its highest level in approximately three decades — driven by BOJ rate-hike speculation and rising oil prices in a global debt selloff. US Treasury Secretary Bessent, meeting Finance Minister Katayama and BOJ Governor Ueda on the G20 sidelines, publicly stated he expects Japan to act to support the yen and signaled US acceptance of a BOJ rate hike, while describing current yen weakness as 'pretty contained.'

So what: A BOJ rate decision at or before the mid-September Monetary Policy Meeting would directly affect the yen, JGB yields, and Japan's debt-servicing costs on a record ¥143 trillion budget request cycle.
