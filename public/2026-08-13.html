Putin makes first-ever visit to Etorofu island; Japan protests, calls it 'absolutely unacceptable'

Russian President Vladimir Putin visited Etorofu (Iturup) island in the Japan-claimed Northern Territories on August 13, his first-ever visit to the disputed chain, asserting Russian sovereignty during a broader Russian Far East trip. PM Takaichi described the visit as incompatible with Japan's position that the islands are an inherent part of Japanese territory, and Japan lodged a formal diplomatic protest.

So what: The visit raises the diplomatic baseline for Japan-Russia relations ahead of the August 15 WWII surrender anniversary and complicates Tokyo's carefully managed posture toward Moscow during the Ukraine conflict.

Pattern: Russia has conducted island-renaming provocations and airspace violations over the Northern Territories-claimed islands previously — the most recent acute prior incident per the regional tracker was a July 25 airspace violation — but a presidential visit to the islands is reported by today's sources as a first.

North Korea fires second ballistic missile in six days; falls west of Hokkaido outside Japan's EEZ

North Korea launched a ballistic missile on August 12, its second in less than a week, reaching approximately 56 miles altitude before falling west of Hokkaido outside Japan's exclusive economic zone; Japan condemned the launch and lodged a formal protest. The launch came days before scheduled major US-South Korea joint military drills, continuing a pattern of pre-exercise DPRK provocations.

So what: The trajectory — falling west of Hokkaido — places the launch in the same general splash zone as the August 7 missile, sustaining an elevated DPRK threat signal as Japan monitors the start of US-ROK exercises.

Pattern: Short- and medium-range ballistic missiles splashing down near Japan's EEZ in the Sea of Japan have recurred across multiple years; North Korea's August 7 launch was its first ballistic test since April, making this back-to-back launch pace the highest in 2026.
