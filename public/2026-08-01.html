Economy-BOJ

US backs Japan yen intervention in unprecedented bilateral FX coordination

The US Treasury notified banks it might intervene in the dollar-yen exchange rate and, per FT reporting confirmed by Reuters and Bloomberg, directly purchased yen alongside Japan in a coordinated action — the first such explicit US participation in Japan-directed FX intervention in recent decades. BOJ current-account data suggest Japan sold approximately ¥8.45 trillion; Reuters estimated total yen-buying at up to $59 billion, driving USD/JPY to a two-month low below 157.50.

So what: Treasury Secretary Bessent and BOJ Governor Ueda are scheduled to meet in late August, likely at the G20, where the durability of this FX coordination framework and any formal currency-policy commitments will be scrutinized.

Economy-BOJ

BOJ holds at 1.0% with hawkish tilt; Ueda flags possible accelerated rate hikes

The BOJ kept its policy rate at 1.0% by an 8-1 vote on July 31, with Governor Ueda warning that core inflation risks are tilted upward and that the pace of rate hikes could accelerate if conditions warrant — language that markets read as keeping a September move alive. The Financial Times reported the BOJ's guidance as the most hawkish accompanying statement of the current tightening cycle.

So what: A September BOJ rate hike, if realized, would compound fiscal pressure on PM Takaichi's government as it pursues a consumption-tax cut and reconstruction spending — directly affecting the Diet's autumn budget and bond-market dynamics.

Japan launches National Intelligence Bureau; China calls it 'another negative move'

Japan's National Intelligence Bureau formally launched on July 31 and convened its inaugural National Intelligence Council meeting, completing a major counterespionage institutional reform. China's foreign ministry immediately characterized the launch as 'another negative move' and urged Japan to 'act prudently,' warning against remilitarization — the second consecutive day of Chinese diplomatic rebuke tied to a Japanese security-reform announcement.

So what: China's coordinated same-day response across CGTN, Global Times, and the foreign ministry press briefing continues the pattern, documented in the tracker since July 31, of Beijing publicly framing Japanese security reforms as provocative — a dynamic that will recur as Japan's Three Strategic Documents review proceeds.

Hudson Institute
Abe's Final Victory: Japan's Defense Revolution and the End of Postwar Pacifism
Japan's post-2022 defense buildup — counterstrike capability, the 2% GDP spending commitment, and institutional reforms — represents the culmination of the strategic agenda Shinzo Abe championed throughout his career, constituting a structural end to postwar pacifism rather than an incremental adjustment.
The Hudson Institute piece argues that Japan's defense revolution should be understood as Abe's posthumous strategic victory, with the December 2022 National Security Strategy and subsequent implementation steps fulfilling policy goals Abe pursued across multiple premierships. The analysis frames the current Takaichi-era defense buildout — including the National Intelligence Bureau launch on July 31 and counterstrike operationalization — as institutional lock-in of that agenda. The piece situates Japan's shift within a broader end of the postwar constitutional-pacifist consensus.
For NSC and DOD analysts, the piece provides a historical-continuity frame for assessing how durable Japan's defense transformation is likely to be across future prime ministers and coalition configurations.
