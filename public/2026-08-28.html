Japan's Narrowing Path to Permanent Residency
Japan's immigration rules continue to restrict foreign workers from accessing permanent residency, limiting the country's capacity to address demographic decline through labor immigration.
The piece examines the structural barriers facing foreign workers seeking permanent residency in Japan, arguing that despite labor shortages and record-low birth rates, the legal pathway remains narrow and bureaucratically difficult. It connects Japan's demographic emergency — underscored by today's news that births rose for the first time in 11 years — to the structural constraints on immigration as a policy lever. The piece notes that without reform, Japan's reliance on a shrinking domestic workforce will intensify.
Relevant to the Takaichi government's economic security and growth agenda, and to the portfolio of Minister for Economic Security Kimi Onoda, who also handles foreign residents policy coordination.
