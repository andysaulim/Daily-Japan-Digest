BOJ holds at 1%, signals early hike possible; Japan-Korea joint yen intervention confirmed

The Bank of Japan kept its policy rate at 1.0% on July 31 and lifted its FY2026 economic outlook while Governor Ueda warned of upside inflation risks and said the pace of rate hikes could accelerate — language the FT characterized as hawkish. The decision came hours after Japan and South Korea conducted a rare joint foreign exchange intervention that pushed USD/JPY below 160, with Treasury Secretary Bessent publicly acknowledging Japan may have intervened.

So what: BOJ's hawkish signal combined with confirmed FX intervention sets up a market test of whether Takaichi's fiscal loosening (food tax cut) and BOJ tightening can coexist without renewed yen volatility or a political showdown.

Reuters / Jiji Press / FT / Bloomberg, Jul 31
Politics-Diet

Takaichi formally announces food consumption tax cut to 1% from April 2027 for two years

PM Takaichi confirmed Japan will reduce the consumption tax on food to 1% from April 2027 for a two-year period, per Kyodo News and Jiji Press, formally launching a policy that drew both public support and sharp LDP internal opposition. Nikkei Asia warned the plan threatens Japan's fiscal future given the existing ¥5 trillion-plus forex special account surplus and the broader debt trajectory.

So what: Legislation must pass through a divided Diet — likely in an autumn extraordinary session — with the timeline and political arithmetic still uncertain given intra-LDP dissent.

Kyodo News / Jiji Press / Nikkei Asia, Jul 31
Defense

Japan launches National Intelligence Bureau; Tomahawk ship-launch test completed

Japan stood up its first National Intelligence Bureau on July 31, with the National Intelligence Council convening its inaugural meeting under Chief Cabinet Secretary Kihara, per Kyodo News and Jiji Press. Separately, the JMSDF conducted the first ship-based Tomahawk cruise missile test launch from JS Chokai in the Pacific, per Japan Times, operationalizing a counterstrike capability range covering North Korea, China, and Russia.

So what: China's foreign ministry immediately characterized both actions as 'negative security policy shifts' and warned against remilitarization, continuing a pattern of Beijing publicly opposing each incremental step in Japan's defense transformation.

International Institute for Strategic Studies (IISS)
Burden sharing beyond budgets: Japan's Official Security Assistance
Japan's Official Security Assistance (OSA) program represents a burden-sharing mechanism that extends beyond the defense budget headline figure to include capacity-building assistance to partner countries.
The IISS analysis examines Japan's OSA framework as a tool for regional security burden-sharing that supplements the headline 2%-of-GDP defense spending target. The piece argues OSA allows Japan to build partner-nation military capacity across the Indo-Pacific, extending deterrence without requiring proportional increases to the JSDF's own force structure. The analysis engages directly with the 2022 National Security Strategy's commitment to expand defense cooperation.
As Japan's Three Strategic Documents undergo their 2026 review, OSA's governance structure and funding baseline are live policy variables for US-Japan alliance burden-sharing discussions.
