DPRK

North Korea fires short-range ballistic missile on Hiroshima anniversary; Kim Yo Jong warns of military options over Japan's strike capabilities

Japan's Defense Ministry confirmed North Korea launched a suspected ballistic missile toward the East Sea on August 6, landing outside Japan's EEZ; the launch came one day after Kim Yo Jong issued a statement warning Pyongyang would pursue additional military options in response to Tokyo's growing counterstrike capabilities, including the Tomahawk sea-launch test. South Korea's military confirmed the short-range ballistic missile launch; Japan filed a protest via MOFA.

So what: The launch falls days before US-ROK joint drills begin, compressing the diplomatic space for any trilateral US-Japan-ROK deterrence signaling and coming as Japan's Three Strategic Documents review — which includes counterstrike doctrine — is underway.

Pattern: North Korea has a documented pattern of launching missiles to coincide with US-ROK exercises and Japan-linked anniversaries; prior EEZ-adjacent launches prompted Japan to accelerate missile-defense procurement discussions.

Politics-Diet

Takaichi attends Hiroshima ceremony as PM for first time; avoids explicit reaffirmation of three non-nuclear principles

PM Sanae Takaichi made her first Hiroshima memorial appearance as head of government on the 81st anniversary of the atomic bombing, emphasizing Japan's commitment to non-nuclear principles without explicitly reaffirming all three, as the government prepares to revise key national security documents later this year. Opposition leaders including DPP head Yuichiro Tamaki criticized the address as insufficient, and South China Morning Post and Anadolu Agency both flagged the non-reaffirmation.

So what: The ambiguity on the three non-nuclear principles, occurring as Japan's National Security Strategy review is active, sets up Diet interpellations and potential diplomatic friction with China and South Korea ahead of the August 9 Nagasaki ceremony.

Economy-BOJ

Cabinet approves food consumption tax cut to 1%; PM Takaichi overrides fiscal objections by invoking election mandate

The Takaichi Cabinet formally approved the plan to cut Japan's consumption tax on food to 1% from the current 8%, effective April 2027 for two years, with PM Takaichi citing her election pledge to silence intra-LDP and opposition opponents. Reuters reported the decision was made despite fiscal concerns; Jiji Press flagged that the cut puts Japan's social security financing framework at a crossroads.

So what: A Diet bill must pass before April 2027 implementation, meaning the measure becomes a political test for the coalition ahead of any future lower-house election, with revenue-impact disputes likely to define Diet proceedings this autumn.

BOJ June minutes show board split on JGB buying; PM Takaichi asked Ueda for bond purchases; Bessent signals rate-hike expectations

The BOJ's released minutes from its June 15–16 MPM show the board debated mounting price risks even as it hiked rates and was split over JGB purchases; Reuters reported separately, citing Jiji, that PM Takaichi asked BOJ Governor Ueda in May to buy more bonds when needed, creating institutional tension over fiscal-finance boundaries. So what: The intersection of PM pressure for bond-buying, Bessent's rate-hike expectations, and ongoing yen interventions sets the terms for the next BOJ monetary policy meeting, with the yen holding at 157 and markets watching for any trigger.

Asia Policy Point
Takaichi's Legislative Strategy Unites Opposition
PM Takaichi's legislative tactics on the food tax cut and imperial succession law have inadvertently consolidated opposition parties against her administration.
The Asia Policy Point analysis argues that Takaichi's approach to pushing contentious legislation—bypassing inter-party consensus-building and relying on the coalition majority—has united otherwise fragmented opposition parties. This dynamic threatens to complicate Diet passage of the food tax cut bill and future security-related legislation. The piece connects declining approval ratings to the legislative strategy rather than policy substance alone.
If the opposition coalition holds together through the food tax cut Diet process, Takaichi faces a more constrained legislative environment for the Three Strategic Documents revision and defense budget requests this autumn.
