Japan's security policy is driven by two distinct and sometimes competing logics — alliance management and autonomous strategic hedging — whose interaction shapes the pace and direction of Tokyo's defense buildup.

The tension between these logics affects decisions on counterstrike capability, defense spending prioritization, and the Three Strategic Documents review. The framework is directly applicable to current debates over Japan's drone strategy revision and missile range cooperation with Australia.
