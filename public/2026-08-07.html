DPRK

North Korea fires short-range ballistic missile on Hiroshima anniversary, first launch since April

North Korea launched at least one short-range ballistic missile toward the East Sea on August 7, the 81st anniversary of the Hiroshima atomic bombing, confirmed by South Korea's military and Japan's Defense Ministry; the projectile landed outside Japan's exclusive economic zone, with no damage confirmed in Japan. The US said the launch posed no immediate threat and stated it was consulting closely with allies; North Korea's state media was silent on the launch.

So what: The launch comes days before the scheduled start of US-South Korea joint military exercises, which North Korea has previously signaled as a provocation trigger, and follows Kim Yo Jong's August 5 statement warning of new military options in response to Japan's Tomahawk test.

Pattern: North Korea has a documented pattern of using the Hiroshima/Nagasaki anniversary period and the eve of US-ROK exercises for missile demonstrations; the August 31, 1998 Taepodong-1 overflight and October 4, 2022 Hwasong-12 overflight of Japan both came in proximity to allied military activities.

Takaichi hedges on non-nuclear principles at Hiroshima, draws opposition criticism and Mainichi editorial rebuke

So what: Takaichi's Yasukuni decision on August 15 and the Nagasaki ceremony on August 9 are the immediate next tests of whether she adjusts or holds this posture under domestic and allied pressure.

Japan discloses record ¥6.27–6.28 trillion single-day yen intervention on April 30; yen surrenders nearly half its gains

Japan's Ministry of Finance confirmed the April 30 foreign exchange intervention totaled ¥6.27–6.28 trillion — the largest single-day operation on record — disclosed in official data reported by NHK, Jiji, and Mainichi. Bloomberg reported the yen has since surrendered nearly half the gains from the combined US-Japan intervention, with USD/JPY trading around 158.30 as of August 7.

So what: The disclosure lands alongside an active September BOJ rate-decision debate, with the IMF's Katz publicly stating the BOJ should continue policy normalization and US Treasury Secretary Bessent reported to have repeatedly pressed the BOJ on rates.

Japan's sweeping intelligence reforms
Japan's newly established National Intelligence Bureau and associated legislative changes represent the most sweeping reform of Japan's intelligence architecture in the postwar period.
The IISS analyzes the structural and legal dimensions of Japan's July 2026 intelligence reforms, centered on the launch of the National Intelligence Bureau.
